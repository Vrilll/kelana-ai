# Japan Destination Playbook

KelanaAI's house guidance for planning Japan: seasons, rail routing and the
rules our designers follow when building a Japan itinerary.

> Rail pass prices and coverage are set by the operators and change. Confirm the
> current fare in the supplier feed before quoting a number. This document
> records how KelanaAI *plans*, not what a ticket costs today.

## 1. When to go

| Window | Conditions | KelanaAI guidance |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Late March to early April | Cherry blossom | Highest demand of the year. Book 5-6 months ahead or plan elsewhere. |
| Late April to early May | Golden Week | Domestic travel peak. Avoid entirely for Standard budgets. |
| June to mid-July | Rainy season | Cheapest. Fine for cities, poor for hiking. |
| Late July and August | Hot and humid | Festivals, but 35C days. Plan indoor afternoons. |
| Late October to early December | Autumn colour | Our default recommendation. Second-highest demand. |
| January and February | Cold, clear, quiet | Best value. Excellent for snow regions and onsen. |

Golden Week and the mid-August Obon week are the two windows where a Standard
budget buys a Backpacker experience. The assistant should say so and suggest
moving the dates.

## 2. Rail routing rules

Japan trips are planned around the rail network, not around road transfers.

**When a nationwide rail pass is worth it.** As a planning rule of thumb, a
nationwide pass pays for itself at roughly **two or more long-distance return
journeys** in its validity window - for example Tokyo to Kyoto and back plus one
more intercity leg. A traveller staying in one region almost never breaks even on
a nationwide pass; a regional pass usually wins.

Because pass pricing has risen faster than point-to-point fares, KelanaAI's
default is now to **price the actual journeys first and only then compare a
pass**, rather than assuming a pass is cheaper. Never put a nationwide pass in an
itinerary without showing the point-to-point comparison.

**IC cards.** Suica and Pasmo work on almost all city transport and in
convenience stores. Budget the IC card top-up inside the local transport slice,
not as an activity. They do not cover Shinkansen reserved seats.

**Reserved seats.** On the Tokaido Shinkansen, reserve seats for any traveller
with a large suitcase - oversized baggage requires a reserved space and turning
up without one is a fine plus a scramble.

## 3. The standard routes we sell

| Route | Nights | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Tokyo only | 4 | Enough for the core districts plus one day trip |
| Kyoto only | 4 | Enough for east and west Kyoto plus Nara |
| Golden Route: Tokyo, Hakone, Kyoto, Osaka | 10 | Our most-booked Japan itinerary |
| Tokyo and Kyoto, no Hakone | 8 | The Standard-budget version of the Golden Route |
| Add Hiroshima and Miyajima | +2 | Only worth it at 10 nights or more |
| Winter add-on: Hakuba or Niseko | +4 | January to early March only |

Minimum for any multi-city Japan trip is **7 nights**. Below that KelanaAI sells
a single-city itinerary instead, because two city moves in under a week means the
traveller spends a third of the trip in transit.

## 4. Daily rhythm rules

1. Temples and shrines in Kyoto open early and fill by 09:30. Schedule the
   headline site first, not after lunch.
2. Never schedule more than **two** ticketed sites in one day. Japan itineraries
   fail from over-packing more than from anything else.
3. Build in one unstructured half-day per 5 nights. Travellers consistently rate
   these the best part of the trip.
4. Department store basements are the highest-value dinner option on Backpacker
   budgets after 19:00, when hot food is discounted.
5. Many museums close on Mondays. Check before placing one on a Monday.

## 6. Practical house rules

- Cash still matters at small shrines, older restaurants and rural stations.
  Tell Backpacker travellers to carry cash for at least one day's food.
- Luggage forwarding between hotels is cheap and removes the worst friction of a
  multi-city trip. Put it in the itinerary as a line item on any route with two
  or more city changes.
- Tattoos still bar entry at many public onsen. If an itinerary includes onsen,
  note private-bath options rather than assuming access.
- Accommodation in Kyoto sells out for autumn colour before Tokyo does. Book
  Kyoto first when both are in the itinerary.
